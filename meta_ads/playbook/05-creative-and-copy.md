## 1. Principles

1. **Creative is the main lever, not the audience.** "creative is the key" (TEST "How small wins compound over time" [10:20]); "this is the most important thing, not your audience" (MC "Audience Types Explained" [25:53]); "It is all down to copy and creative now rather than the audience level" (LWU "Add Site Links to Your Ads" [0:00]). With broad or Advantage+ targeting, Meta finds the audience from what the ad says and shows: "How you create the ad now is about how you create an audience" (MC "Audience Types Explained" [10:25]); see also LWU "Paid vs Organic" [5:38].
2. **The first 3 seconds decide everything.** "Stop the scroll in 3 seconds or less" (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks", lesson text). Only about 20% of viewers are left at 4 seconds and about 10-13% at 8 seconds (LG "What creative type should you use?" [2:26]-[3:14]).
3. **Call out the right person.** "Meta isn't learning from you calling out your ideal customer" (LWU "Breaking Down a Winning Ad" [0:00]). One word in the opening line changed CPL from about 14.99 to 6 (same lesson [2:52], [6:36]).
4. **Volume and iteration beat reinvention.** "You can get one video or one static and we can find so many different iterations" (TEST "Why most ads fail" [3:59]). You need "a lot more [creative] than you need to even six months ago" (MC "Audience Types Explained" [24:58]).
5. **Problem → solution is the default angle.** Cold creative shows a problem the viewer recognises, then the fix. "Problem solution videos. This is what is my favorite type of cold outreach" (ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [5:59]); "this is going for a problem solution style. Now I love this type of copy" (LOCAL "Writing Ads That Get Clicks" [4:38]). Talk about the problem, not features (ECOM "Understanding product/market fit" [9:51]).
6. **Simple and native beats polished.** Phone video is fine (LG "What creative type should you use?" [10:55]). The best UGC doesn't "even look like an ad" (ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [9:07]). Face-to-camera with a text-overlay hook is "by far 90%" of what wins (TEST "Why most ads fail" [4:47]).
7. **Test one variable at a time, and let data pick winners.** "we only test one variable at a time. Really, really important" (TEST "One variable at a time" [0:00]). Don't stay attached to a creative you like (LWU "Stop Using the Wrong 'Bait'", lesson text).
8. **Ads fatigue; plan the refresh.** "Expect creative fatigue. Don't build one ad, press live and leave it" (WELCOME "Step 5"); fatigue is worse in small local radius audiences (MC "Inside the Meta Ecosystem" [6:13], [8:32]).
9. **[Clinic adaptation]** For a PT clinic, every hook and line of copy must also pass Meta's personal-attributes policy (CL-POL-01). The course's own best clinic hooks ("Doctor said your labs are fine, but you don't feel fine") are written in a style that asserts the viewer's health state. This playbook rewrites them (see R-HK-02 and R-CPY-01).

## 2. Rules

### Hooks (R-HK)

### R-HK-01: Land the hook in the first 3 seconds, everywhere it appears
- When: Every ad. The "hook" is the first line of primary text, the headline, the on-image text of a static, and the first words or overlay of a video (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [0:00]).
- Do: Put the call-out and the reason to keep watching in the first 3 seconds. Front-load the whole message into the first ~4 seconds of video. Put the offer or CTA early, not at the end. Make short cuts and iterations of long videos because most viewers have gone by about 20 seconds.
- Don't: Build up slowly with a story. Story-style videos hold attention longer but "haven't really got the call to actions in them" and convert worse (LG "What creative type should you use?" [2:26]).
- Numbers: Hook in 2-3 s (MC "Audience Types Explained" [24:11]); value clear in 3 s, aim to land it in 3-4 s (ECOM "Understanding product/market fit" [12:06]; "Typical eCom funnel structure" [5:06]); ~20% of viewers left at 4 s, ~10-13% after 8 s (LG "What creative type should you use?" [2:26]-[3:14]); viewers dropping off by about 18-20 s (MC "Audience Types Explained" [24:58]).
- Source: as cited.

### R-HK-02: Call out the audience without asserting their condition
- When: Writing any hook, overlay, headline or first line of copy for the clinic.
- Do: Open by naming who the ad is for in the first words, and match the call-out to the real market. If CPL is high or quality poor, check whether the call-out is too niche or aimed at the wrong group, and broaden it slightly (LWU "Breaking Down a Winning Ad" [5:48]-[8:54]). The course's winning script pattern is: who + problem + specific outcome + timeframe + CTA ("Parents, do you have a child in reception or year one and you want them to jump up to two reading levels in as little as 12 weeks?...").
- Don't: Copy the course's clinic hooks as written. "Doctor said your labs are fine, but you don't feel fine" (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [3:22]) and "still feeling awful after being told everything looks good" (MC "Audience Types Explained" [24:11]) tell the viewer what they feel.
- Numbers: "Ex-pats parents" → "Parents" cut cost per lead from about 14.99 to about 6 ($/£ both used in captions) (LWU "Breaking Down a Winning Ad" [2:52], [6:36]).
- Source: as cited; TEST "How small wins compound over time" [3:56], [5:51] (US functional medicine clinic hooks "no answers", "still feel awful").
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** CL-POL-01. Meta's personal-attributes policy bars ads that assert or imply a person's health condition, typically by addressing "you" about the condition **(general best practice, not from course; check Meta's current Advertising Standards)**. Rules of thumb:
  - Name the audience by place, activity or life stage, not by diagnosis: "[Town] runners", "weekend golfers", "parents who lift toddlers all day".
  - Talk about the clinic, the service, or "people" / "many of our patients", not "your pain".
  - First-person stories from an authorised patient or the clinician are fine in tone (testimonials need CL-HIP-05).
  - Avoid questions that ask the viewer about a condition ("Do you have sciatica?", "Is your knee pain...?", "Back pain stopping you?").
  - Rewrite examples:

  | Course-style (don't run) | Compliant rewrite (example) |
  |---|---|
  | "Doctor said your scans are fine, but you don't feel fine." | "Many of our patients were told their scans looked 'normal'. Here's how our physical therapists in [Town] look for what scans miss." |
  | "Still in pain after months of rest?" | "Rest isn't always the whole answer. A 60-minute PT evaluation in [Town] looks at how you move, not just where it hurts." *(no condition asserted; adjust visit length to the real offer)* |
  | "Struggling with knee pain on the stairs?" | "For [Town] runners and walkers who want to get back to their usual routes: one-to-one physical therapy, no referral needed." *(check direct-access rules for your state before saying "no referral")* |
  | "POV: your back finally stopped hurting" | "POV: you found a PT in [Town] who spends the whole hour with you." |

  Run new wording past the person who signs off on ads before launch. If Meta rejects an ad, rewrite rather than appeal with the same framing.

### R-HK-03: Overlay a text hook on talking-to-camera video
- When: Any video, especially clinician talking head, interview or podcast-style clips.
- Do: Film vertically on a phone. Leave space above the head. Put the hook text above the head or below the chest. Keep the hook overlay on screen for the whole video and add burned-in captions. A second overlay later in the video can carry the CTA.
- Don't: Put the hook where the face or the placement UI will cover it (see R-CR-10).
- Numbers: Overlay "for the first three to four seconds" gives "a massive uplift" (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [0:51]); later the same course keeps it up the whole video (LG "What creative type should you use?" [2:26]).
- Source: as cited; WELCOME "Canva - Design all you ads in here!" [1:47] (second timed overlay); LWU "Breaking Down a Winning Ad" [6:36] (overlay stating the promise).

### R-HK-04: Draft many hooks with AI, then edit them yourself
- When: Starting a hook test or refreshing a tired ad.
- Do: Ask ChatGPT for about 10 short hooks to overlay for the first 3 seconds. "POV" and problem-question styles work. If it returns one angle only (e.g. all "POV"), ask for different angles. Always edit into your own tone of voice. Good hooks are specific and show the value before the click; highlight words like "free".
- Don't: Paste AI output straight into ads.
- Numbers: "write me 10 shortish hooks" (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [1:40]); 4 new hooks + the current one (LWU "Canva Tutorial: Test Hooks That Work" [0:00]).
- Source: LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [1:40]-[3:22]; LWU "Canva Tutorial: Test Hooks That Work" [0:00].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Run every AI hook through CL-POL-01 (R-HK-02); AI tools default to "Are you suffering from...?" phrasing. Never put patient names or details into the prompt (CL-HIP-05) **(general best practice, not from course)**.

### R-HK-05: Test hooks one at a time on the same creative
- When: You have a video or static and want to find the message that works.
- Do: Keep video, primary text, headline and CTA identical; change only the overlay hook. Order: (1) one video × 3 hooks; (2) the winning hook across 3 videos; (3) winning video + hook × 3 copy variants. Build variants by duplicating the ad and using "Change media/video" (not delete and re-upload) so other settings stay put. Name each ad by the hook ("Video 2 | Hook 3 | Copy 1", or the hook text itself) so you can read results without clicking in.
- Don't: Test hook and copy and video at once. Don't tick multiple text options or creative enhancements in a test ad (R-CPY-03, R-CR-11).
- Numbers: "if you haven't got loads of budgets, I would just test three" (TEST "One variable at a time" [0:47]); "one video, get five different hooked for text overlay" when starting from scratch (TEST "What to test: Hook, Creative, Audience" [8:31]).
- Source: TEST "One variable at a time" [0:47]-[5:59]; "How to structure a basic testing campaign" [6:11]-[8:52]; "What to test" [9:26]; LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [3:22].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Pick the winning hook on cost per booked evaluation and show rate, not CPL alone (CL-KPI-01). Use CPL and CTR to cut obvious losers early; confirm winners in the weekly GoHighLevel/IntakeQ reconciliation (CL-KPI-03). Budget, kill thresholds and spend-before-judging rules are in the testing file.

### R-HK-06: Keep a winning hook and iterate its styling before replacing it
- When: A hook is winning, or a winner starts to tire.
- Do: Keep it running. Make "iterations": same video, same hook, different styling (coloured highlight, arrow, coloured key word, different text placement). Test these in a duplicated ad set alongside the original. Try the winning hook on new videos.
- Don't: Assume a winning hook must be retired quickly. One clinic hook "just works every single time... We can't beat it" (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [3:22]-[4:21]).
- Source: TEST "When to test again" [0:51]-[3:23]; LG "What creative type should you use?" [4:05]; LWU "Canva Tutorial" [0:00] ("change it before we get creative fatigue").

### Creative (R-CR)

### R-CR-01: Produce creative continuously and iterate from what works
- When: Always. This is the main ongoing job in the account.
- Do: Keep fresh creative coming. Make more of what works ("We're just going to make more of what works", LG "Key Metrics to Watch" [5:57]). Start from one video or static and spin out iterations. Keep a performing ad even if it is off-brand or not pretty.
- Don't: Reinvent from scratch each time. Don't permanently scrap a creative after one failed test; "just because you've got a video working in one, doesn't mean it's going to work in another", and the reverse (TEST "What to test" [5:53]).
- Numbers: new iterations "every couple of weeks" (MC "Inside the Meta Ecosystem" [1:26]); an off-brand static ran 4 months at 12x ROAS vs ~8x for polished ads (ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [1:48]-[3:24]) (e-commerce example).
- Source: as cited; TEST "Why most ads fail" [2:00]-[3:59]; MC "Audience Types Explained" [24:58].

### R-CR-02: Run both video and static
- When: Building any new ad set.
- Do: Have at least one video and one static. Video introduces the brand and the person; statics are fast to read and "do still work really, really well". If you can't make video yet, start with statics and move to video as you learn what works. A phone is enough; voiceover or hands-only video is fine if you won't show your face.
- Don't: Wait for professional production.
- Numbers: a client's talking-to-camera video "completely beaten the statics" (LG "What creative type should you use?" [0:49]).
- Source: LG "What creative type should you use?" [0:00]-[4:05], [10:55]; ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [5:06]; LWU "The NEW way to run Ads on a low budget" (lesson text: "Transition from static ads to video").

### R-CR-03: Make face-to-camera video the core format, from a short script
- When: Planning video.
- Do: Film the clinician (or owner) talking to camera. Script: hook/call-out → problem → outcome → "click the link below" → sign-off. A teleprompter app is fine if delivery looks natural. Add a text overlay stating the promise. Clinic case study openers: map pin + staff waving or walking into the clinic (R-CR-05).
- Numbers: this style is "by far 90%" of winners (TEST "Why most ads fail" [4:47]).
- Source: TEST "Why most ads fail" [3:59]-[4:47]; LWU "Breaking Down a Winning Ad" [5:48]-[6:36]; LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [0:51].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The script's "problem" line follows CL-POL-01: describe the problem in general or third person ("a lot of people we see..."), not "you have...". Clinician-led content avoids the authorisation work that patient testimonials need (CL-HIP-05).

### R-CR-04: Use UGC, testimonials and demos, with written authorization
- When: Diversifying beyond talking head, and in retargeting.
- Do: Use phone-shot, unpolished content with text overlay. Testimonials that state "this was the problem I had and it stopped because of this" work well. Use demos and benefit-led ads too. Cold traffic: problem/solution and UGC. Warm/retargeting: social proof, testimonials, offers.
- Don't: Write "reviews" yourself "as if people have written them" (suggested in ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [7:39]). **Do not follow this**: it is fabricated reviews (FTC rule on fake reviews and Meta policy) **(general best practice, not from course)**.
- Source: ECOM "Understanding product/market fit" [11:01]-[12:06]; ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [7:39]-[9:07]; ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [5:59]-[7:46], [11:14]; LWU "The NEW way to run Ads on a low budget" and "£50+ a day?" (lesson text).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** CL-HIP-05: any patient testimonial, photo, video, name or story needs written HIPAA authorization before use, kept on file. The course's "ask happy customers via DM" method is not enough on its own. Also:
  - A patient's first-person story ("I was told my scans were fine...") is acceptable in tone; the ad copy around it must still not say "you" have the condition (CL-POL-01).
  - Don't imply typical results; avoid cure or guarantee language **(general best practice, not from course)**.
  - Staff demos (exercise, how an evaluation works) need no patient authorization if no patient is identifiable.

### R-CR-05: Make local creative unmistakably local
- When: Every clinic ad (radius-targeted).
- Do: Name the town in the hook in the first 2-3 seconds of video, in statics, and in the ad copy. Use footage or images of a street or landmark locals recognise, or a map-pin graphic. In the clinic case study, a map pin at the start ("functional medicine in Portland, Maine") plus staff waving worked best. Exclude nearby areas whose name doesn't match your hook.
- Numbers: location on screen 2-3 s (LG "Local Creative that converts" [2:11]); "the map at the beginning, the little pin works really, really well" (TEST "How small wins compound over time" [3:56]); local hooks like "South Portland, Maine" make viewers feel "This advert is halfway to being for me" (MC "Audience Types Explained" [23:18]).
- Source: as cited; LOCAL "Visual is what stops the scroll!" [1:02]-[3:00]; LOCAL "Setting Up Your Local Lead Campaign" [3:15]-[4:04] (exclusions).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** CL-LOC-01. A radius audience sees the same ads often; local variants (different landmark, pin placement, town name in the ad set's sub-areas) are the cheapest way to refresh.

### R-CR-06: Structure local offer videos: place → offer → what's included → action
- When: Making local service videos and statics.
- Do: 1) where you're based (2-3 s); 2) the offer; 3) what's included, shown gradually; 4) end on the action (fill in the form). Include the offer in every creative. Use standout colours and light background music (many watch sound-off). Satisfying process footage stops the scroll. Make 3-4 variants of the same concept (text placement, pin, different clip).
- Numbers: roof-cleaner best video £5-7 CPL, other variants under £15; 4 statics made, a bold red "20% off" won (LG "Local Creative that converts" [2:11]-[3:00]).
- Source: LG "Local Creative that converts" [0:00]-[3:00]; LOCAL "Visual is what stops the scroll!" [0:00]-[3:00].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Offers such as a free screen or reduced first visit must be cleared for payer rules before use; free or discounted services can raise anti-inducement issues with Medicare/Medicaid patients **(general best practice, not from course)**. Don't promise outcomes in the "what's included" section. Judge variants on booked evaluations, not CPL alone (CL-KPI-01).

### R-CR-07: Statics: brand, call-out and offer in one image
- When: Making any static.
- Do: Get the brand, the call-out and the offer into the one image (course example: "The Ads Clinic, fix your failing Facebook ads, join our free school"). Iterate by emphasising the key word: underline, circle, bold or colour "FREE".
- Source: LG "What creative type should you use?" [4:05].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The call-out line follows CL-POL-01 ("[Town] physical therapy, 1-hour one-to-one visits", not "Fix your back pain").

### R-CR-08: Test "organic text post" statics
- When: You need a new static cheaply, for lead gen.
- Do: On the Facebook page, start a post, pick a coloured background, type a hook and a short CTA, stay within the background-text character limit, and screenshot it (wait for the blinking cursor to disappear). Don't publish the post. Make several colour/text variants and test them.
- Numbers: CTR 2.2%+; CPM down 19%. Tested on lead gen, not e-commerce (TEST "Static FB Text Creative", lesson text and [0:00]).
- Source: TEST "Static FB Text Creative" [0:00]-[1:03].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The plain-text format makes the words the whole ad, so CL-POL-01 matters more here.

### R-CR-09: Set a custom video thumbnail in every placement group
- When: Every video ad.
- Do: At ad level → creative → placement groups → edit → Thumbnail. Upload an image or pick a frame. Do it for all three placement groups so the first frame is consistent.
- Don't: Leave Meta's auto frame if it's a bad angle.
- Source: LWU "Creating Thumbnails for Video Ads" [0:00].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The course suggests showing "the end result... or it might be a before". Don't use before/after imagery for a health service; Meta's health policies restrict it **(general best practice, not from course)**. Use the clinician, the clinic exterior or the town instead.

### R-CR-10: Design one vertical asset that survives every placement
- When: Making any creative (Canva templates are the course's default tool).
- Do: Use one vertical format, with key information inside the central square so it survives square feed crops. Keep overlay text inside safe-zone borders and out of the top and bottom zones (Stories/Reels UI). Keep media in original aspect ratios; don't let Meta force a crop. Preview every placement (square feed, vertical Reels/Stories) before publishing. Leave placements on Advantage+ (automatic); only restrict placements when the creative can't be adapted.
- Source: WELCOME "Canva - Design all you ads in here!" [0:00]; LWU "Canva Tutorial" [0:00]; LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [8:02]; MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" [7:44], [12:27]; MC "Build an Lead Gen Ad" [8:32].
- Note: The course never gives pixel dimensions for safe zones. The "Winning Placements in Ads Manager" lesson promises placement advice, but its transcript is a columns tutorial (LWU).

### R-CR-11: Turn Advantage+ creative enhancements off, and re-check after every upload
- When: Every ad build and every creative swap.
- Do: Switch off Advantage+ creative enhancements (music, visual touch-ups, text overlays, image animation, enhanced CTA, image generation, AI text). They reset to on whenever a creative is re-uploaded. While there, confirm the product catalog shows "not connected" (Meta can auto-attach one, even another account's).
- Don't: "Apply all" of Meta's AI text suggestions. Review them one by one if you use any.
- Source: MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" [10:09]-[12:27] ("I want you to keep your enhancements off"); MC "Build an Lead Gen Ad" [10:09]-[11:46]; LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [16:21] ("they make the ads look scruffy"); LWU "Use Product Catalogues the Right Way" [1:16]-[3:44]; TEST "How to structure a basic testing campaign" [8:52].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** AI text rewrites can turn compliant copy into "you"-framed copy; keep text improvements off too (CL-POL-01).

### R-CR-12: Use flexible ads only when you don't need to know which creative won
- When: Choosing between one creative per ad vs a flexible ad.
- Do: For tests, put each creative in its own ad. Use a Flexible ad (ad set → Format → More options → Flexible) only on small budgets when account-level results matter more than creative learnings; load 3-5 media, not 10. If your account still has Dynamic Creative, Breakdown → Dynamic creative element shows the winning video/text/headline; Flexible does not.
- Numbers: 1-10 media allowed; 3-5 "under a £50... a day budget" (LWU "Building a Flexible Ad" [0:48]); Dynamic Creative: up to 10 media, 5 headlines, 5 primary texts (TEST "What to test" [1:36]).
- Source: LWU "Building a Flexible Ad" [0:00]-[1:42]; LG "What creative type should you use?" [6:39]-[8:17]; TEST "How to find out your winning ad copy" [0:00]-[0:54].

### R-CR-13: Treat site links as an optional later test
- When: Only after the core ads work, and only on single-image or single-video ads.
- Do: If testing: add at least 4 site links (Meta won't show fewer), each with a clear display label and optional thumbnail. Drop them if they don't help.
- Don't: Expect them in carousel or flexible ads; they can't be used there.
- Numbers: at least 4 links (LWU "Add Site Links to Your Ads" [1:50]-[3:10]).
- Source: LWU "Add Site Links to Your Ads" [0:00]-[4:01]; MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" [7:44] ("site links... That's all we're going a bit too advanced. We can leave all those off").
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Point site links only at generic pages (home, about, location, FAQ); never at condition-specific, booking or intake pages (CL-HIP-06, CL-HIP-01). On an instant-form ad, the form is the conversion path; site links pull people away from it, so default off.

### R-CR-14: Reuse winning posts to keep their social proof
- When: Moving a winning test ad into a main campaign, or putting money behind a good organic post.
- Do: Winning test ad → main campaign: Preview → "Facebook post with comments" → copy the post ID at the end of the URL → new ad → "Use existing post" → Enter post ID. Only the CTA and destination (landing page or lead form) can change. Organic post → ad: the course uses the Engagement objective, because not all objectives accept an existing post; the original text can't be edited.
- Source: TEST "Move WINNING Test Ad into Master Campaign" [0:00] (lesson text: "this keeps all previous ad social proof!"); LWU "Turn Organic Posts into Paid Ads" [0:00]-[1:52].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** For clinic organic posts, use "Use existing post" inside the Leads campaign with an instant form rather than an Engagement campaign, so spend still goes to leads (CL-RES-01). Moderate comments: people may disclose conditions; hide them and never reply in a way that confirms someone is a patient (CL-HIP-05). The post's text must already meet CL-POL-01, because it can't be edited.

### R-CR-15: Research the Ad Library for ideas, not clones
- When: Before a creative sprint, or when stuck.
- Do: Open Meta's Ad Library; set country to All and ad category to All ads; search competitors by name or by topic, or open a Page → About → Page transparency. Look at creative, copy, headline, destination, and the instant-form questions they ask. It shows no results data.
- Don't: Copy someone else's "winning ad"; it "rarely works for your account" (LWU "Stop Using the Wrong 'Bait'", lesson text).
- Source: LWU "Utilising Meta's Ad Libary" [0:00]-[2:53]; ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [18:55] (look at how successful businesses in your sector do it).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Look at other PT, chiro and sports-medicine advertisers, local and national. Many competitor ads use "you"-framed condition hooks; don't take those as proof the framing is safe (CL-POL-01).

### R-CR-16: Diversify creative concepts, not just variations
- When: Planning the creative mix, especially as spend grows.
- Do: Run several concept types in the account: talking head, UGC/testimonial, demo, offer-led, benefit-led, local (pin/landmark), text-post static. Iterations of one concept are not diversification. With broad/Advantage+ targeting, different concepts reach different "pockets" of the audience; Meta may push one creative and that is expected.
- Numbers: the course's structure lessons separate under-£50/day accounts (simple structure, test concepts) from £50+/day accounts (separate testing and control campaigns) (LWU "The NEW way to run Ads on a low budget", "£50+ a day?", lesson text).
- Source: LWU "The NEW way to run Ads on a low budget" and "£50+ a day?" (lesson text only: "master creative diversification with UGC, testimonials, and benefit-led ads"); TEST "What to test" [6:54] ("finds its own little pocket"); LWU "What to do if Meta spends on ONE AD only" (lesson text).
- Note: The course says Andromeda changes creative testing, but the videos that explain how have no transcript. This rule is the most this playbook can support from the source.

### R-CR-17: Watch fatigue weekly and refresh before results slide
- When: Weekly review of every live ad.
- Do: Watch frequency (impressions ÷ reach). Rotate out or replace an ad when frequency passes 2; treat a climb toward 3 as a sure sign results will get pricier. Refresh with a hook test on the same strong video before fatigue sets in. Add new iterations every couple of weeks. Keep retargeting budgets small so frequency stays down.
- Numbers: "my frequency went above two" → ad turned off (TEST "Scaling rules" [8:33]); "we don't want our frequency to go to [three]" (LWU "Paid vs Organic" [3:07]-[4:35]); new creative "every couple of weeks" (MC "Inside the Meta Ecosystem" [1:26]).
- Source: as cited; LWU "Canva Tutorial" [0:00]; MC "Inside the Meta Ecosystem" [6:13], [8:32] (40-mile clinic: show the same audience "different things").
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** CL-LOC-01. A radius audience fatigues faster than the national examples in the course. Check frequency, CTR trend and cost per booked evaluation every week; refresh creative on fatigue signals rather than widening targeting; keep a creative bank (R-CR-18) so refreshes are ready. Budget pacing for small audiences is in CL-LOC-02.

### R-CR-18: Keep a creative bank and tracker
- When: From the first ad onward.
- Do: Keep a tracker (the course provides a "CREATIVE TRACKER" Google Sheet). Columns: creative name, hook text, format (video/static), date created, destination (instant form vs landing page), status, launch date, cost per lead, hook rate and hold rate (video), leads, CTR, link to the asset folder. Sort winners to the top (green), then losers, then sections for to-be-tested, working hooks and new filming ideas. Name ads exactly like their files. After a couple of months, revive an old winner by duplicating the original ad (not switching the old one back on); "most of the time it will start up where it left off".
- Numbers: hook rate (3-second plays ÷ impressions) 25%+ is "a really good marker"; hold rate (ThruPlays ÷ impressions) about 10% (LWU "Tracking Video Success: Hook & Hold Rate" [1:54]).
- Source: TEST "Building a 'top performers' creative bank" [0:00]-[0:47]; LWU "Campaign Naming Tips" [1:50].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Add columns for booked evaluations, shows and new patients per ad from the weekly reconciliation (CL-KPI-01, CL-KPI-03), a policy-check column (CL-POL-01), and, for any patient content, authorization on file Y/N with expiry (CL-HIP-05). Keep patient names out of file names and ad names.

### Copy (R-CPY)

### R-CPY-01: Write problem → solution → what's included → CTA
- When: Primary text for cold ads.
- Do: Lead with the problem the reader recognises, then the solution, then the list of services or what's included, then the CTA. Talk about pain points and outcomes, not features or technique; features belong on the landing page or form. Let the copy filter out poor fits.
- Source: LOCAL "Writing Ads That Get Clicks" [4:38]; ECOM "Understanding product/market fit" [9:51]; ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [5:59]; LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [0:00] (problem/solution copy); LG course intro (filter time-wasters).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** CL-POL-01 is the key policy risk here. State the problem about people in general, or in the clinic's voice, never as a fact about the reader. Example:
  - Course style (don't run): "Is your back pain stopping you from playing with your kids? Our PT fixes the cause, not just the symptoms. Book now."
  - Compliant rewrite: "Lifting kids, long drives and desk days catch up with a lot of people in [Town]. At Movement Solutions, a physical therapist spends a full one-to-one visit finding what's driving the problem and builds a plan you can do at home. What's included: [evaluation], [hands-on treatment], [home program]. Limited new-patient spots this month. Tap 'Book now' to request a time."
  - Avoid "fear" framing ("imagine if this happened to you", ECOM "Understanding product/market fit" [11:01]) for health; it tends to imply the condition and can be rejected **(general best practice, not from course)**.

### R-CPY-02: Use 2-3 primary texts and headlines in live ads, but one of each in tests
- When: Building any ad.
- Do: In a live (non-test) ad, give at least 2 primary texts and 2 headlines (up to 3 each); Meta rotates them and shifts to the best. A good starting set is three ChatGPT copy styles: problem/solution, professional, casual/friendly. In a test ad, use exactly one primary text and one headline so only the tested variable changes. Leave the description blank (rarely shown).
- Don't: Load many variants on a low budget. Don't tick the "multiple text options" boxes in a single-variable test.
- Numbers: "if you've got a low budget, I wouldn't choose too many of these... always choose two" (MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" [7:44]); 3 primary texts (LOCAL "Writing Ads That Get Clicks" [4:38]); 4-5 copies in a dynamic/flexible ad (LG "What creative type should you use?" [5:01]).
- Source: as cited; TEST "One variable at a time" [1:38]-[2:24]; MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" [11:10] (description).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Every variant must pass CL-POL-01 on its own, because Meta can show any of them alone.

### R-CPY-03: Draft copy with AI from a simple prompt, then edit
- When: Writing primary text.
- Do: Prompt: "Create me some Facebook ads copy for my [service] business, which includes [list of services]". Take the three variations, fix the CTA to match the destination, and edit to your voice. The course offers the prompt as a download.
- Source: LOCAL "Writing Ads That Get Clicks" [1:01]-[5:39]; LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [1:40] (AI for hooks and copy).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Remove any "you have/are suffering from" lines the AI adds (CL-POL-01). Never include patient details in prompts **(general best practice, not from course)**.

### R-CPY-04: Make the CTA match the destination and the offer
- When: Choosing the CTA button and writing the closing line.
- Do: Pick the button that matches what happens next: Book now, Sign up, Get quote, Learn more, Download. Say what happens: "complete the form to get a quote", not "Message now" when the ad opens a form. Add honest urgency ("limited slots this month", "while spots last").
- Don't: Use urgency that isn't true.
- Source: LOCAL "Writing Ads That Get Clicks" [3:45]; LOCAL "Setting Up Your Local Lead Campaign" [4:54]; LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [16:21]; MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" [12:27]; MC "Build an Lead Gen Ad" [9:20].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The default clinic destination is a native instant form (CL-RES-01), so the copy says "tap Book now to request a time" or "fill in the short form and we'll call you", and the form's end screen links to booking. Only promise a fast call-back if follow-up really happens within minutes (CL-KPI-02).

### R-CPY-05: Keep message match from hook to destination
- When: Every ad.
- Do: The hook, offer and copy should match what the person sees after the click: the instant-form intro headline, or the landing page headline. "we're not looking for people to see the ad and then click and end up on your website going, what am I doing here?" (TEST "What to test" [2:31]).
- Source: as cited; LG "Landing Page" [2:35]-[4:01]; LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [2:26]-[3:14].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** If a landing page is used, keep its URL slug generic (not /sciatica) (CL-HIP-01, CL-HIP-06).

### R-CPY-06: Retargeting copy reminds of value; it never says what you saw them look at
- When: Writing copy for warm/retargeting ads.
- Do: Remind fence-sitters of the value and offer ("hey, you were on the fence..."), and lean on social proof and availability.
- Don't: Personalise with what they viewed ("we saw you looking at [product]", ECOM "Catalog Sales vs Manual Campaigns" [16:40]-[18:40]).
- Source: MC "TOF, MOF, BOF: Funnel Explained" [11:04]; ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [11:14].
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** "We saw you looking at back-pain treatment" implies knowledge of a health interest: do not follow for the clinic (CL-POL-01, CL-HIP-06). Retarget only from engagement or generic-page audiences, and write the copy as if to a cold reader.

## 3. Open questions / contradictions in the course

1. **Creative enhancements.** MC "Build an Ecomm Ad" says keep all enhancements off; MC "Build an Lead Gen Ad" [10:09]-[11:46] turns "Text improvements" on ("I've changed my mind") while still telling beginners to keep it off. **Default: all off**, including text improvements, because AI rewrites can break CL-POL-01 (R-CR-11).
2. **How many text variants.** "Always choose two" (MC), exactly one in tests (TEST "One variable at a time"), three ChatGPT texts (LOCAL), 4-5 in dynamic/flexible (LG), 5 × 5 in dynamic creative (TEST "What to test"). **Default: one primary text and one headline in test ads; 2-3 of each in live ads** (R-CPY-02).
3. **3 hooks or 5?** "just test three" on a small budget (TEST "One variable at a time" [0:47]) vs one video with five hooks (TEST "What to test" [8:31]; LWU "Canva Tutorial"). **Default: 3** at clinic budgets, because local budgets are small (CL-LOC-02).
4. **Hook overlay duration.** "first three to four seconds" (LG "Attention-grabbing Hooks" [0:51]) vs kept up the whole video (LG "What creative type should you use?" [2:26]). Hook timing also varies: 2-3 s, 3 s, 3-4 s. **Default: the hook lands within 3 s and the overlay stays up for the whole video** (R-HK-01, R-HK-03).
5. **Flexible vs separate ads.** Flexible is recommended for small budgets (LWU "Building a Flexible Ad") but hides which creative won, which every testing lesson needs; Dynamic Creative is "disappearing". MC turns flexible media off in one build and on in another. **Default: one creative per ad for tests; flexible only for an evergreen ad where creative-level learning isn't needed** (R-CR-12).
6. **Site links.** MC says leave them off as "too advanced"; LWU says add at least 4 and "jump on the bandwagon". **Default: off; optional later test on single image/video ads pointing only to generic pages** (R-CR-13).
7. **The course's best clinic hooks breach Meta policy.** The flagship clinic hooks ("Doctor said your labs are fine, but you don't feel fine"; "still feeling awful") are second-person statements about health. The course never mentions the personal-attributes policy. **Default: compliant rewrites per CL-POL-01** (R-HK-02, R-CPY-01). Expect some loss of punch; the call-out still works through place, activity and the clinician's voice.
8. **Frequency threshold.** Turn off above 2 (TEST "Scaling rules" [8:33]) vs "we don't want our frequency to go to [three]" (LWU "Paid vs Organic"). **Default: flag at 2, act before 3**, reviewed weekly because a radius audience saturates fast (R-CR-17, CL-LOC-01).
9. **Kill fast vs don't scrap.** "cut throat" kills after a few expensive leads (TEST "How small wins compound" [2:52]) vs never permanently scrap a creative that failed once (TEST "What to test" [5:53]). **Default: pause the ad, keep the asset in the creative bank, and retest it later with a different hook** (R-CR-01, R-CR-18).
10. **Winner metric.** Lessons pick creative winners by CPL or "highest ROI, CTR and best cost per result" (TEST "How to find out your winning ad copy", lesson text), and note the best CTR ad isn't always the best CPL ad (LG "Key Metrics to Watch" [6:48]). **Default: cost per booked evaluation and show rate decide (CL-KPI-01); CPL and CTR are early filters.**
11. **Organic posts as ads.** LWU uses the Engagement objective; TEST moves posts by post ID into the main (conversion) campaign. **Default: "Use existing post" inside the Leads/instant-form campaign** (R-CR-14, CL-RES-01).
12. **Ad Library vs "don't copy winners".** LWU teaches "spying" on competitors; the "Wrong 'Bait'" lesson says copying someone else's winning ad rarely works. **Default: use the Ad Library for angles, formats and form ideas; don't clone** (R-CR-15).
13. **Thumbnails and testimonials.** The course suggests "before" thumbnails and "problem → result" testimonials, and once suggests self-written reviews. **Default: no before/after imagery; testimonials only with written authorization (CL-HIP-05); never fabricate reviews** (R-CR-04, R-CR-09).
14. **Video vs story.** Video builds familiarity with the person, but story-led videos "haven't really got the call to actions" and convert worse (LG "What creative type should you use?" [2:26]-[3:14]). **Default: short, CTA-early videos for lead ads; story-style only as a tested concept.**
15. **Andromeda-era guidance is thin.** The lessons that explain post-Andromeda structure and creative diversification have no transcript. R-CR-16 rests on lesson blurbs plus the testing lessons. Treat it as provisional and revisit if transcripts become available.
