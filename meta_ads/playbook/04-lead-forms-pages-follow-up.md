## 1. Principles

1. **Lead gen is a journey: awareness > value > trust > action.** These are stages in the customer's head, not Meta objectives. "I only want you to think of one thing, lead generation objective, lead objective" (LG "Awareness > Value > Trust > Action" [3:18]).
2. **Prove you can help them by actually helping them.** Give value before you ask for anything. "prove you can help them by actually helping them" (LG "Awareness > Value > Trust > Action" [0:00]).
3. **The offer comes first.** No ad fixes a weak offer. "If you've got a product that is underpriced, if you've got a service that people don't really want, you're just not gonna get an ad that's just gonna change the world for you" (WELCOME "Step 1 - Welcome to The Ads Clinic" [4:30]).
4. **The funnel is: lead magnet → form → calendar → call → nurture.** People who don't book go into a flow of emails and texts with CTAs (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [6:18]-[7:21]).
5. **What happens after the click is often the bottleneck.** "It's not just about getting an ad creative and an ad that works, it's about what happens after you've got the click" (WELCOME "Step 1 - Welcome to The Ads Clinic" [0:50]). Slow sign-up processes and "red tape" can sink good ads (TEST "Why most ads fail" [2:00]-[3:10]).
6. **Speed wins.** "when you get a lead in, you need to be on that lead, as soon as possible" (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [15:35]).
7. **Friction trades volume for quality.** Short forms and no pre-booking questions give more leads. Higher-intent forms, rich creative forms and SMS verification give fewer, better leads. The course leans to low friction plus fast follow-up (LG "What does success look like?" [1:30]; LG "Email/SMS follow-up and nurture" [5:06]).
8. **Judge leads by what they turn into, not by CPL alone.** £15.60 per lead is called "a good lead cost" because of the business it wins (LG "What does success look like?" [1:30]).
9. **Don't build everything up front.** Launch, learn what works, then add pieces. "I would not advise going out and building this whole funnel before you know what's working" (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [0:00]).

## 2. Rules

### Offers and lead magnets (R-OFR)

### R-OFR-01: Lead with a value-first offer, not "give us your details"
- When: Designing what the ad and form promise.
- Do: Offer something useful in return for contact details: a free call or review, a free community, an e-book or PDF, a sample. Then nurture toward the paid service.
- Don't: Go from ad straight to a form that asks for lots of details and gives nothing back. "you just asking too much of a prospect and they're just not willing to give this away."
- Source: (LG "Awareness > Value > Trust > Action" [0:00], [8:07]); (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [0:54]-[4:10]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Typical value-first offers are a free phone consult or screen, or a guide. Keep the offer wording general (for example "free 15-minute call with a physical therapist") so the form topic does not reveal a condition (CL-HIP-03, CL-POL-01).

### R-OFR-02: Make the first offer strong enough to act on
- When: Choosing the size of the entry offer.
- Do: Accept a small loss on the first acquisition if the back end pays. A bigger offer drives far more sign-ups.
- Don't: Use a token offer everyone else uses ("you're all giving 10% off").
- Numbers: "10% off" vs "25% off" (example). A local roof cleaner's "20% off" static won out of 4.
- Source: (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [0:54]-[1:58]); (LG "Local Creative that converts" [3:00]); (ECOM "Typical eCom funnel structure" [11:14]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Measure the offer on cost per booked evaluation and cost per new patient, not on lead volume (CL-KPI-01). Free or discounted services can raise inducement or payer-contract issues; check with compliance before offering (general best practice, not from course).

### R-OFR-03: Keep the offer identical from ad to form or page
- When: Every ad.
- Do: Use the same hook, offer and wording in the ad, the form intro or the landing page headline. Give each offer or service its own form or landing page.
- Don't: Send people somewhere that makes them think "what am I doing here?"
- Source: (LG "Landing Page" [2:35]-[4:01]); (TEST "What to test: Hook, Creative, Audience" [2:31]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** One form per offer is fine. Name forms and pages by offer, not by condition (see R-LP-02, CL-HIP-01).

### R-OFR-04: Check the offer before blaming the ads
- When: Leads are expensive or don't convert.
- Do: Ask whether the offer is clear, priced right for the market and wanted by the target customer. Test whether people convert without a big discount.
- Source: (WELCOME "Step 1 - Welcome to The Ads Clinic" [4:30]); (ECOM "Understanding product/market fit" lesson text, [13:32]-[14:26]).

### R-OFR-05: Start simple and add funnel pieces as you learn
- When: Launching a new lead campaign.
- Do: Start with one form, one offer and a basic follow-up. Add steps once you know where people drop out.
- Don't: Spend weeks building the whole funnel first.
- Source: (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [0:00]); (WELCOME "Go High Level - CRM on STEROIDS!" [3:28]: "even if you did it really simple with a nice one, two, three first emails").

### Instant forms (R-FRM)

### R-FRM-01: Default to an instant form; use a landing page only when you need one
- When: Choosing the conversion location for a lead ad set.
- Do: For local service businesses, send traffic to an instant form: "I really recommend going to a form purely because you're going to get more results at a lower cost." Keep the destination choice at ad set level so you can test a form ad set against a landing-page ad set in one campaign.
- Don't: Assume one is better without testing. "you could increase performance by using instant forms, you could, but you may not, so we need to test these things."
- Source: (LOCAL "Visual is what stops the scroll!" [2:11]); (LG "Local Creative that converts" [2:11]); (LG "Setting up a Meta Landing page campaign" [1:45], [4:24]); (MC "Build an Lead Gen Ad" [0:51]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The clinic uses the Leads objective with native instant forms as the default. Website lead optimization may not be available to a health advertiser (CL-RES-01), and a landing page with a pixel adds HIPAA risk (CL-HIP-01).

Decision guide (built from the lessons above; the course gives no firm rule beyond "test"):

| Situation | Use |
|---|---|
| Local service, want volume at low cost | Instant form, More volume |
| Lead quality is poor on More volume | Duplicate to Higher intent and test |
| Need more explanation but no good landing page | Rich creative instant form |
| Strong, message-matched landing page with a booking calendar and working tracking | Landing page (not the clinic default, see R-LP-07) |

### R-FRM-02: Build the form from the ad's Destination section, before you build the ad
- When: Creating a new form.
- Do: Go to ad level > Destination > Create form. This gives "more capabilities and more options" than the Instant Forms library. Build the form first, then build the ad around it.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [0:00]); (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [0:00]); (LOCAL "How to create an Instant Form" [0:00]).

### R-FRM-03: Start with More volume; move to Higher intent only if quality is poor
- When: Choosing the form type.
- Do: Start with More volume (quick fill). If lead quality is poor, duplicate the form and the ad, switch the copy to Higher intent (adds a review screen and swipe-to-submit), and test.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [0:00]-[0:49], [12:30]-[13:20]): "I would always recommend just starting with a more volume form. See how that data comes through. If you want some more high quality leads, move to a higher intent."
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Define "poor quality" by GHL contact rate and booking rate, not by gut feel (CL-KPI-02).

### R-FRM-04: Use a rich creative form as a stop-gap landing page
- When: You need more explanation than a short form, but don't have a strong landing page.
- Do: Choose the first image carefully, because it sets the colour theme and you can't change it. Add a headline, overview, three benefits and story sections (how it works, services, social proof, accreditations). Each testimonial card needs an image.
- Numbers: three benefits.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - Rich creative" [0:00]-[4:04]); (LOCAL "Create friction with longer forms" [0:00]-[4:55]).
- Note: Rich creative has no Higher-intent swipe. The course says the amount of content already filters for quality (LG "Instant form - Rich creative" [4:55]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Patient testimonials, names and photos need written HIPAA authorization (CL-HIP-05). The copy must not assert the viewer has a condition (CL-POL-01).

### R-FRM-05: Turn off Flexible form delivery, or lock what Meta may change
- When: Building any form.
- Do: Switch Flexible form delivery off, or keep it on and untick the elements Meta must not remove or change.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [1:38]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Locking matters for the clinic, because Meta must not add fields that CL-HIP-03 rules out.

### R-FRM-06: Make the intro say exactly what the form is for and what they get
- When: Writing the form's intro screen.
- Do: Put the offer in the greeting headline (example: "book a free 30-minute consultation"). In the description, say what they are filling in, what they will get, and what the questions are about. Some people open forms by accident, so this keeps the right people going. The background image is optional and "doesn't impact delivery". If you use one, keep text and logos clear of the top crop.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [1:38]-[3:14]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** State the offer, not a condition: "Book a free call with a physical therapist", not "Get help for your sciatica" (CL-POL-01).

### R-FRM-07: Ask few questions; qualify with one or two practical ones and a clean exit
- When: Adding questions to the form.
- Do: Keep the form close to "open". The instructor went "straight back to basics" with one or two qualifiers. Use multiple choice with conditional logic: route a "no" to a custom end page ("sorry, our services aren't right for you") or close the form. Ask questions that help the follow-up call (the roofer's form asks property type).
- Don't: Stack questions. Each question costs volume (see R-LP-06).
- Numbers: instructor's form has 2 questions (running ads yes/no; monthly spend). Example kick-out: spend "below 1000".
- Source: (LG "What does success look like?" [1:30]); (LG "Landing pages vs Lead Forms" [1:19]); (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [3:14]-[5:48]); (LG "Building a Local Campaign" [4:54]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Form questions are limited to contact details and non-clinical qualifiers (CL-HIP-03). Meta stores every answer with the person's identity.
  - OK: preferred clinic location, best time to call, "are you within X miles of [town]?", "in-person or virtual?".
  - Not OK: pain area, diagnosis, injury, surgery, condition, symptoms, date of birth, "what brings you in?" free text.
  - Insurance or self-pay questions: get a compliance decision first. Until then, ask on the call or in IntakeQ.
  - Collect all clinical information in IntakeQ after booking.

### R-FRM-08: Require email and phone; add no other contact fields
- When: Setting up the contact section.
- Do: Add one plain line on how you will use their data (example: "We will use your details to contact you about..."). Keep the pre-filled full name, email and phone, and make email and phone required, because that is how you follow up.
- Don't: Add extra fields "because you can". Meta offers address, WhatsApp, date of birth and work info.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [6:40]-[7:32]); (LOCAL "How to create an Instant Form" [6:40]-[7:32]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** No date of birth, address or other identifiers on the Meta form (CL-HIP-03). IntakeQ collects these.

### R-FRM-09: Link a real privacy policy
- When: Every form. It is mandatory.
- Do: Link to a privacy policy page on your own site that says why you collect the data and what you do with it. The link text can be changed. Add a custom disclaimer only for things like competitions or giveaways.
- Source: (LG "Landing pages vs Lead Forms" [1:19]); (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [7:32]-[8:20]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The linked page should be a generic privacy page with no Meta pixel on it (CL-HIP-01). Have it reviewed so it covers SMS and email follow-up consent (general best practice, not from course).

### R-FRM-10: Use the completion screen to let people book now
- When: Setting up the ending page.
- Do: Give the main path a next step: a link to the booking calendar so they can book the same day, a file download, or a call button. Give the disqualified path its own end page. A PDF lead magnet can be uploaded and delivered on the end screen.
- Don't: Use the in-form "appointment request" question. A calendar on your site from the end screen is "more intuitive". Don't make people wait for an email.
- Numbers: about 1 in 3 of the instructor's leads book straight from the end screen. The rest go into a nurture of 3 emails + 3 SMS. This is one account, not a benchmark.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [5:48], [8:20]-[9:07]); (LG "What does success look like?" [1:30]); (LG "Landing pages vs Lead Forms" [1:19]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The end-screen button links to the IntakeQ/PracticeQ online booking page for the offer (for example a free call or evaluation). Use a neutral URL with no condition in the path or parameters. Do not put a Meta pixel on the booking or confirmation pages (CL-HIP-01). Count end-screen bookings in the weekly reconciliation (CL-KPI-03).
  - Suggested end-screen text: "Thanks. Our team will call you shortly. Want to pick a time now? Tap below." (adapted from the instructor's wording).

### R-FRM-11: Only connect Messenger chat if someone can reply instantly
- When: Meta offers "chat with leads" after the form.
- Do: Enable it only if you have the staff and response times to keep the conversation warm.
- Don't: Get people excited and then reply hours later.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [9:07]-[9:59]); (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [14:18]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Leave it off. People describe their symptoms in Messenger, which puts clinical information on Meta. Follow-up runs through GHL instead (CL-HIP-03, CL-KPI-02).

### R-FRM-12: Set sharing to Open; clean up field names and parameters
- When: The form's Settings step.
- Do: Set Sharing to **Open** so people who are tagged in or shared the ad can also submit. Set the language. Retype field names without underscores so exports read cleanly. Add tracking parameters if a CRM needs them to route leads.
- Source: (LG "Landing pages vs Lead Forms" [0:00]); (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [9:59]-[11:38]); (LG "Instant form - Rich creative" [5:48]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Parameters and field names must not name a condition or a condition-specific service line, such as `campaign=back-pain` (CL-HIP-01). Neutral offer names are fine.

### R-FRM-13: Preview the whole form before you press Create
- When: Finishing a form.
- Do: Click through every page and every logic path as a user would. "you cannot edit a form, you have to duplicate it and redo whatever you want to do." Forms can be deleted but not edited.
- Source: (LG "Instant form - More volume and higher intent" [11:38]-[12:30]); (LG "Instant form - Rich creative" [4:55]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Submit one test lead and check that it lands in GHL with the right fields and tags before the ad goes live (CL-KPI-03).

### R-FRM-14: Don't switch on SMS verification by default
- When: Meta offers the option to text the lead a 4-digit code before the form submits.
- Do: Leave it off unless lead quality is clearly a problem, then test it.
- Don't: Expect it to be free. It raises quality, but in the instructor's tests so few people completed it that cost per lead became very expensive.
- Numbers: 4-digit code. He says instant-form lead quality was "really good" as of July 2025.
- Source: (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [11:31]-[14:18]).

### R-FRM-15: Instant-form ads don't need the website pixel
- When: Ad-level Tracking settings on an instant-form ad.
- Do: Know that the instructor turns "Website events" on "out of habit" even though there is "not really that much need" on an instant-form ad.
- Source: (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [17:23]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Do not follow for the clinic. Leave website events off on instant-form ads unless the pixel setup has passed review (CL-HIP-01, CL-RES-01).

### Landing pages (R-LP)

### R-LP-01: Build and check the page in mobile view
- When: Building or editing any landing page.
- Do: Design and test in mobile view first. Check that everything fits on the phone screen.
- Numbers: "90%" of visitors are on mobile (LG); "95% or more" (ECOM).
- Source: (LG "Landing Page" [0:00]); (ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [4:17]).

### R-LP-02: One page per offer or service, matching the ad
- When: Running more than one offer or service.
- Do: Duplicate a working page and change the offer and URL slug (example: `/landing-50-off`). Keep the hook, offer and copy consistent with the ad, or "we're just creating a bottleneck for ourselves". Send people to the specific page, never the home page.
- Source: (LG "Landing Page" [2:35]-[4:01]); (ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [0:00]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Use neutral slugs (`/free-call`, `/first-visit`), never condition slugs (`/sciatica`). No pixel on condition-specific pages, and no retargeting audiences built from them (CL-HIP-01, CL-HIP-06).

### R-LP-03: Put the full pitch above the fold on mobile
- When: Laying out the first mobile screen.
- Do: Fit these on the first phone screen:
  - a headline naming what they get
  - the offer
  - how it works
  - a risk reducer ("no contract")
  - scarcity ("only four spaces left")
  - the CTA button
  - a testimonial video
- Source: (LG "Landing Page" [4:01]-[6:00]); (ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [3:05]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Only use scarcity that is true (general best practice, not from course). Testimonial videos need written HIPAA authorization (CL-HIP-05).

### R-LP-04: Below the fold, repeat the CTA after every block
- When: Building the rest of the page.
- Do: Stack short blocks, each followed by the same button: testimonial, problem/solution, "meet your mentor", what we cover, more testimonials ("Still sitting on the fence?"). Answer FAQs in compact sections, such as accordions. Put testimonials near the CTA.
- Source: (LG "Landing Page" [6:00]-[7:41], [10:31]); (ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [7:39], [16:06]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Useful clinic FAQs are cost and insurance, location and parking, what happens at the first visit, and how soon they can be seen (adapted from ECOM). Testimonials follow CL-HIP-05.

### R-LP-05: Write about the prospect's problem first, your business last
- When: Writing page copy.
- Do: List their pain points (the instructor uses four), then how you fix them. Mention your company lower down. "talk about your prospect, what your prospect's pain points are and how you're going to fix them."
- Don't: Copy your website's "about us" style. "A website never really talks about the client."
- Source: (LG "Landing Page" [9:31]-[10:31]).
- Note: The instructor says to copy his clean, simple page (white and grey, short, calendar embed, "due to high demand spaces are limited"), which has worked for about five years (LG "Landing Page" [7:41]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Describe problems in general or third-person terms. The ad that links to the page must follow CL-POL-01.

### R-LP-06: Let people book first; ask questions after
- When: Deciding whether to put qualifying questions before the calendar.
- Do: Put the booking calendar first. After booking, send a link to a short questionnaire by email and SMS.
- Don't: Put pre-qualification questions before the booking. The instructor found that people "become less and less patient" and the questions stopped them booking.
- Numbers: 5 questions.
- Source: (LG "Email/SMS follow-up and nurture" [4:19]-[5:55]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The questionnaire is the IntakeQ intake form, sent by IntakeQ after booking. This is where clinical questions belong (CL-HIP-03).

### R-LP-07: Landing-page conversion tracking (course setup; not for the clinic)
- When: Running a Leads campaign to a website.
- Do (course): Install the pixel and a Lead event that fires after the person submits or books (thank-you page, not page load). Use "Maximize number of conversions" on that event. Never optimize for link clicks or landing page views ("your results are going to be awful"). Check ad-level Website events is on, as it is "sometimes not on by default".
- Source: (LG "Setting up a Meta Landing page campaign" [0:00], [5:12], [13:50]-[14:43]); (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [5:09]); (LG "Landing pages vs Lead Forms" [3:27]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Do not follow for the clinic. Lower-funnel website events may be unavailable to a health advertiser (CL-RES-01). A Lead or Schedule event on a booking or thank-you page tells Meta that a person booked a clinical visit (CL-HIP-01). Don't use "bypass" tracking to get around this (CL-RES-02). If the clinic ever runs a landing page, measure it outside Meta (CL-KPI-03).

### Follow-up and lead quality (R-FU)

### R-FU-01: Have follow-up running before the ads go live
- When: Before launch.
- Do: Set up automatic email and SMS for leads you can't reach by phone, even if it is only "one, two, three first emails". Then "you can then press go on your ads, knowing that they're going to be looked after and that person isn't going to be wasted."
- Source: (WELCOME "Go High Level - CRM on STEROIDS!" [0:00], [3:28]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The new-lead GHL workflow and the IntakeQ booking link must both be tested with a real test lead before launch (CL-KPI-02, CL-KPI-03).

### R-FU-02: Deliver leads to the CRM instantly; never rely on CSV downloads
- When: Setting up lead delivery.
- Do: Pick one of the course's two routes:
  - **GHL native:** GHL > Integrations > "Facebook form fields mapping". Select the form, map first name, surname, phone, email and custom questions, and turn the status on. Leads land in GHL Contacts.
  - **Zapier:** Facebook Lead Ads "New Lead" (Page + form) → Google Sheets "Create spreadsheet row" → Gmail "Send email" to the owner. Facebook Lead Ads is a premium Zapier app, so it needs a paid plan.
- Do: Give yourself (and any agency) Lead access in Business settings. If the integration keeps switching itself off, give your user full control of the Page, including leads access, then switch it back on.
- Don't: Download leads from Ads Manager each morning. The CSV is "scruffy" and slow.
- Don't: Set up Meta's new in-ad "CRM integration" yet: "I would just leave this, ignore it."
- Source: (WELCOME "Go High Level - CRM on STEROIDS!" [0:52]-[1:41]); (LOCAL "Instantly Receive Your Leads by Email" [0:00]-[14:10]); (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [3:47], [18:54]); (LG "Building a Local Campaign" lesson text); (MC "What is Business Manager?" [7:57]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Every tool that touches lead data must be HIPAA-eligible with a signed BAA, or not used (CL-HIP-04).
  - Prefer GHL's native form mapping on a GHL plan covered by a BAA.
  - Use Zapier, Google Sheets or Gmail only if each one has a BAA. Otherwise skip the Zapier route.
  - Data flows one way, Meta → GHL. Do not use Meta's CRM integration or any lead-stage sync back to Meta (CL-RES-03).

### R-FU-03: Contact every lead within minutes, on every channel
- When: A lead arrives.
- Do: Get in front of the lead "as quickly as possible": call, SMS, email, and GHL ringless voicemail. If nobody can respond instantly, push leads to self-book on the calendar instead. Send anything you promised (a guide, a callback) at once.
- Source: (LG "Setting up a Meta Lead Form campaign" [15:35]-[16:21]); (ECOM "Importance of product pages, reviews, UGC" [5:34]: "They don't even bother me sending me that 10% off code ... So I'll just end up going somewhere else").
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Speed-to-lead is a core KPI. Track minutes to first contact, contact rate and booking rate in GHL (CL-KPI-02). The repo already has an after-hours and busy-hours GHL lead agent with its own cadence (`docs/LEAD_AGENT_SETUP.md`). Keep the GHL workflow and that agent in step so leads don't get duplicate messages. SMS needs consent and opt-out handling (general best practice, not from course).

### R-FU-04: Use tags to trigger workflows and pipeline stages to track outcomes
- When: Setting up the CRM.
- Do: Tag each contact by what they did or answered (for example "review call booked"). Let tags trigger workflows. Move contacts through pipeline stages: booked, no show, offer presented, won.
- Source: (LG "Email/SMS follow-up and nurture" [1:48]-[2:37], [9:15]-[10:06]); (WELCOME "Go High Level - CRM on STEROIDS!" [1:41]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Suggested stages: new lead → contacted → evaluation booked (IntakeQ) → showed → plan of care started, plus no-show and not a fit. These feed the weekly reconciliation by campaign and ad (CL-KPI-03). Tags and stages stay inside GHL and are never sent back to Meta (CL-RES-03, CL-HIP-02).

### R-FU-05: Run a booked-appointment sequence that confirms and reconfirms
- When: Someone books.
- Do: Follow the instructor's GHL timing:
  - **Immediately:** confirmation email and SMS. Say exactly what format the meeting is ("This will be a Zoom call, not a phone call"). Include the meeting link and an add-to-calendar link.
  - **+3 min:** email and SMS: "did you complete the short questions after booking?" with the link (5 questions).
  - **+60 min:** "Yes, you're booked" reconfirmation ("If you didn't get it, please let me know").
  - **Then:** a longer value email that answers a common objection.
- Numbers: 0 min, 3 min, 60 min. A 10-minute wait was shown and then removed.
- Source: (LG "Email/SMS follow-up and nurture" [2:37]-[6:45]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Split the job so patients don't get duplicates (CL-KPI-02, CL-KPI-03):
  - **IntakeQ/PracticeQ:** booking confirmation, appointment reminders, and the intake forms (the "+3 min questionnaire" step).
  - **GHL:** the pre-booking nurture, plus the "+60 min you're booked" style reconfirmation if IntakeQ does not already cover it.
  - Messages state the visit type and location: in person at [address] or virtual, and what to bring and wear. This is the clinic version of "Zoom call, not a phone call".
  - The course does not cover reminder timing before the visit. A 24-hour and a same-day reminder is common practice (general best practice, not from course).

### R-FU-06: Put non-bookers into a short nurture with a booking CTA in every message
- When: A lead fills the form but doesn't book.
- Do: Send a nurture of emails and texts with CTAs, "so at any point they can come back". Start simple: a day-1 welcome email, an SMS ("Hey {first name}, [name] here from [business], just saw you..."), with Wait steps between (example: 1 hour). Track which non-bookers later book.
- Numbers: instructor uses 3 emails + 3 SMS. Example wait: 1 hour. The course gives no timing for the rest of the sequence.
- Source: (LG "What does success look like?" [1:30]); (LG "Importance of lead magnets, forms, booking flows" [7:21]); (WELCOME "Go High Level - CRM on STEROIDS!" [1:41]-[3:28]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Build this as a GHL workflow that stops as soon as the person books. Every message links to the IntakeQ booking page. Messages must not name a condition the lead never told you about (CL-KPI-02, CL-HIP-03).

### R-FU-07: Run a no-show sequence and keep the booking gap short
- When: Someone misses their appointment.
- Do: Send an email and an SMS ("missed our call, let's reschedule"), wait a day, email again, and keep going. Move the contact to a no-show stage.
- Do: Keep the time between lead and appointment short. "when you get bookings coming in too late, a lot of people forget that they booked and don't turn into calls." One account cut spend by about 50% (from about 250 to 100 a day) once the calendar was full.
- Source: (LG "Email/SMS follow-up and nurture" [8:26]); (LWU "Unlock Facebooks's Hidden Ad Feature" [0:00]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Mark the no-show in IntakeQ and trigger the GHL no-show workflow from it. Track show rate per campaign and ad in the weekly reconciliation (CL-KPI-01, CL-KPI-03). If the first available evaluation is too far out, slow spend rather than book people weeks ahead (CL-LOC-02).

### R-FU-08: Follow up after the first visit if they haven't committed
- When: The offer was presented but not taken.
- Do: Same day: a recap email. +1 day: a testimonial story. +2 days: another case study. Take payment on the call when you can.
- Numbers: same day, +1 day, +2 days.
- Source: (LG "Email/SMS follow-up and nurture" [6:45]-[8:26]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The clinic version is a patient who had the evaluation but didn't book the plan of care. Run this in GHL or IntakeQ, never through Meta. Stories need written authorization (CL-HIP-05). Track plan-of-care starts per campaign (CL-KPI-03).

### R-FU-09: Judge lead quality downstream, not by CPL
- When: Reviewing campaigns.
- Do: Look at booking rate, show rate, close rate, client lifetime and ROI. A higher CPL is fine if those leads close. Get feedback on lead quality from whoever takes the calls. Bookings made soon after launch are a sign of qualified leads.
- Numbers: £15.60 CPL called good because clients stay about 4 months. MC example: 26 leads, 16 calls (61%), 6 sales. LWU example: 27 leads at "$/£14.99" (the captions use both currencies), 5 closed; a new hook gave 5 leads at about £6, 3 booked within a day.
- Source: (LG "What does success look like?" [1:30]); (MC "Inside the Meta Ecosystem" [7:21]); (LWU "Breaking Down a Winning Ad" [2:52], [6:36]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** The clinic judges by cost per booked evaluation, show rate and cost per new patient. CPL is diagnostic only (CL-KPI-01). Quality data comes from GHL and IntakeQ and is reconciled weekly outside Meta (CL-KPI-03). Never upload it to Meta (CL-RES-03, CL-HIP-01).

### R-FU-10: Scale only as fast as the team and the calendar can absorb
- When: Considering a budget increase.
- Do: Scale only as fast as you can handle the leads, with staff to take the calls. Most small businesses don't need huge daily spend.
- Source: (TEST "Scaling rules" [0:48]-[1:44]); (LWU "Unlock Facebooks's Hidden Ad Feature" [0:00]).
- Clinic: **[Clinic adaptation]** Before raising budget, check GHL speed-to-lead and IntakeQ evaluation availability. If either is slipping, hold budget (CL-KPI-02, CL-LOC-02).

## 3. Open questions / contradictions in the course

1. **Instant form vs landing page.**
   - The local lessons say a form gives "more results at a lower cost".
   - MC says forms "could" perform better but must be tested.
   - The instructor's longest-running winner was a landing page.
   - **Default here:** instant form. The health restriction and HIPAA risk of a pixeled landing page make the landing-page route a poor fit for the clinic (CL-RES-01, CL-HIP-01).
2. **Qualify vs minimise friction.**
   - The form lessons praise conditional-logic qualifying and kick-outs.
   - The instructor himself went back to a near-"open" form, and removed pre-booking questions from his landing page because they cut bookings.
   - **Default here:** one or two non-clinical qualifiers, with clinical questions in IntakeQ after booking (R-FRM-07, R-LP-06). This also follows CL-HIP-03.
3. **In-form appointment request vs calendar link.**
   - Both are shown.
   - The same lesson prefers a calendar link from the end screen.
   - **Default here:** an IntakeQ booking link on the completion screen (R-FRM-10).
4. **Form sharing default.**
   - One lesson says the default is Restricted ("very weird") and you must open it.
   - The form-build lesson calls Open "up to you entirely".
   - **Default here:** Open (R-FRM-12).
5. **Website events on instant-form ads.**
   - The Lead Form lesson turns them on "out of habit".
   - The landing-page recap is garbled ("I turn this off as a nice turn this on as a nice to have" [sic]).
   - **Default here:** off for the clinic (R-FRM-15, CL-HIP-01).
6. **SMS verification.** It is called "a fantastic idea" for quality but not used, because volume collapses. **Default here:** off, test only if quality is bad (R-FRM-14).
7. **Messenger chat after the form.**
   - The Lead Form lesson says "This one's good ... working pretty well", but the instructor doesn't use it because he can't reply instantly.
   - The form lesson says only use it with strong staffing.
   - **Default here:** off for the clinic, for staffing and privacy reasons (R-FRM-11).
8. **Pixel "required" for the Leads objective.**
   - MC and LOCAL say you can't use Leads without a dataset.
   - The same courses build Leads → Instant forms campaigns that need no pixel.
   - **Default here:** instant forms with no pixel dependency (CL-RES-01).
9. **When the website Lead event fires.**
   - LG says after submit or booking.
   - MC first shows a button click, then says URL-based tracking after booking is more accurate.
   - This doesn't matter for the clinic, because it doesn't use website lead events (R-LP-07).
10. **Zapier pricing.** LOCAL says "I think you can get a free version", then says the free version doesn't allow this Zap. **Treat it as a paid plan.** For the clinic, the BAA question (CL-HIP-04) matters more than the price.
11. **Where the course is thin.**
    - It gives exact timings only for the booked-call sequence (0 / 3 / 60 min) and the post-call sequence (same day, +1 day, +2 days).
    - The non-booker nurture is just "3 emails + 3 SMS" with no timing. The no-show flow is "wait a day ... repeat" with no end point.
    - It does not cover pre-appointment reminders, show-rate benchmarks, or SMS consent and opt-out rules.
    - The playbook marks anything added on these as general best practice. The clinic's own timings should come from its GHL and IntakeQ data.
12. **"1 in 3 book from the end screen"** is one account (a free 30-minute consultancy call), not a benchmark. Don't use it as a target for the clinic until the clinic has its own numbers (CL-KPI-03).
